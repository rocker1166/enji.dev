Karmyug AI collects and labels training data for AI companies: speech, text, images and video, recorded by real people on real devices in their own language. Workers sign up, pass a short test and an ID check, record tasks on their phone, and get paid for every approved task.

I was the only engineer. I designed and built all four apps and the API, from the first commit on 22 Sep 2026 to the GCP launch on 22 Oct.

## The worker app

A phone-first PWA. New workers go through a checklist (agreements, profile, a qualification test, ID check with a selfie taken in the page), then claim tasks with a 30-minute hold so two people never record the same prompt.

The recording room checks audio before upload: 16-bit WAV, length, loudness, clipping and silence. Conversation tasks let 2 to 8 people record on one phone. Earnings, history and payout requests are all in the app.

## Quality and fraud

Bad data is worse than no data, so quality runs in layers:

1. **In the browser** before upload: format, length, loudness, clipping, silence.
2. **On the server**: speech present, reading speed, duplicate audio.
3. **A Python service** (FastAPI + faster-whisper) for language ID, word error rate against the script and signal-to-noise.

Fraud signals flag duplicate audio, shared devices, shared IPs and VPNs. Reviewers get a queue with audio playback, approve or reject with a reason, and can bulk-approve by rule.

## The admin panel

Projects with pay rates, quotas and pilot batches; task upload by CSV or audio; workers, vendors and clients; KYC; payouts with TDS; a languages directory; email campaigns and automations; a CMS for the public site; an audit log; and dataset export as a ZIP for the client.

## Launch on GCP

Production runs on Google Cloud in Mumbai: four Cloud Run services, Cloud SQL Postgres 17 with daily backups and point-in-time recovery, Cloud Storage, a global HTTPS load balancer with Cloud CDN, Secret Manager and Cloud Scheduler.

## What I learned

Launch day is a load test you don't control. Building the checks into the browser saved the server from thousands of bad uploads, and the dry-run load test (80 workers recording non-stop) is why sign-up day was boring in the good way.
